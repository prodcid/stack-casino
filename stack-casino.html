How betting works

READING THE ODDS

Odds are decimal. Multiply your stake by the odds to get your total return — that includes your stake back.

10.00 at 2.50 returns 25.00
That's your 10.00 back plus 15.00 profit.

Lower odds mean it's more likely. Divide 1 by the odds for a rough chance: 2.00 is about 50%, 4.00 about 25%, 10.0 about 10%.

THE MARKETS

Match resultHome win, draw, or away win. The draw is a real outcome, not a refund — it loses your bet on either team.
Over/Under 2.5Total goals by both teams. Over needs 3 or more, under needs 2 or fewer. The .5 exists so it can never tie.
Both to scoreBoth teams score at least one. A 3–0 is a losing bet on "yes" no matter how many goals there were.
Correct scoreThe exact final score. Hardest to hit, which is why it pays the most.

SINGLES vs ACCUMULATORS

A single is one bet on one outcome. Three selections as singles means three separate bets, each winning or losing on its own.

An accumulator rolls selections into one bet. The odds multiply, so the return gets big fast — but every leg has to win. One loss and the whole ticket is gone.

Three legs at 2.00, 2.29 and 2.63 → about 12.0 combined.
10.00 returns roughly 120.00, but all three must land.

Most books quietly take their cut on every leg, so a 5-fold there returns far less than a single. This one doesn't — an accumulator here pays the same 97% as a single. You're taking more variance, not worse value. Legs from the same match are blocked, because those results affect each other.

LIVE ODDS

Once a match kicks off the prices move every minute. A goal for the favourite shortens their price (turns green); going behind drifts it (turns red).

That's where the value is. Backing a good team that's 1–0 down gets you a much bigger price — you just need them to score twice.

Markets that are already decided get suspended. Once it's 1–1, "both teams to score — yes" is a certainty, and betting on a certainty can only lose you money.

CASH OUT

While a bet is still running you can take a cash out offer instead of waiting for the result. It rises when your bet is winning and falls when it isn't.

Use it to lock in profit when you're ahead, or to get something back when a leg has gone wrong.

Real books shave a margin off cash-out offers to make leaving early cost you. This one pays the true current value, so cashing out and holding on are worth exactly the same on average. Take it whenever you'd rather have the certainty.

THE HOUSE EDGE

Every market here returns 97% over the long run — every bet type, every price, accumulators included. For every 100 staked you'd expect 97 back on average.

There's no bet on this page with better odds than another. Correct score isn't a worse deal than match result, it's just rarer and pays more. Pick what you enjoy watching.

A FEW HONEST POINTERS

Accumulators are the most fun and the least likely to land — treat them as a punt, not a plan. Correct score is close to a lottery. And chasing losses with bigger stakes doesn't change the 97%, it just gets you there faster.

Betting on the fight

READING THE ODDS

Odds are decimal. Stake × odds = your total return, stake included.

10.00 at 2.50 returns 25.00 — your 10.00 back plus 15.00 profit.

Lower odds means more likely. 1 ÷ odds gives you a rough chance: 2.00 is about 50%, 5.00 about 20%.

“THE DISTANCE” — WHAT IT MEANS

This is the one bit of boxing jargon worth knowing. The distance is the full scheduled length of the fight — here, all 3 rounds.

Goes all 3Nobody gets stopped. The final bell rings and the judges' scorecards decide it. Also called "going the distance".
Ends earlySomeone is knocked out or stopped before the final bell. Also called "inside the distance".

Those two cover every possible fight between them, so one of them always wins.

HOW A FIGHT ENDS

KnockdownA fighter hits the canvas and takes a count. They can get up — a knockdown on its own doesn't end anything.
Stoppage / KOThey can't continue, or go down three times in one round. Fight over on the spot.
DecisionAll 3 rounds completed. Each round is scored 10–9 to whoever did more damage, or 10–8 if they floored their man. Highest total wins.
DrawLevel scorecards. Rare, but real — and it beats both fighters, so a bet on either one loses.

THE MARKETS

WinnerWhose hand gets raised, or the draw.
How it endsStopped early, or all 3 rounds. See above.
Stoppage roundWhich round the stoppage comes in. All three lose if the fight goes the full distance — you're betting it ends early and picking when.
How they winWinner and method in one bet. Harder to hit, so it pays more than just picking the winner.

WATCHING THE ODDS MOVE

Prices update every few seconds as damage lands. A fighter getting worn down drifts (turns red); the one doing the damage shortens (turns green). A knockdown swings everything hard.

Watch the health and stamina bars in the header — a fighter on empty stamina gets hit far more easily, and that's usually visible before the odds fully catch up.

Markets that can no longer happen get suspended. Once round 1 is over, "stoppage in round 1" is gone.

CASH OUT

Take the current value of an open bet instead of waiting for the result. It rises when your bet is winning and falls when it isn't, and the button shows you the profit or loss in green or red.

No margin is shaved off cash-outs here, so taking it and holding on are worth the same on average. Take it whenever you'd rather have the certainty.

THE ODDS ARE HONEST

Every price comes from running this exact fight simulation thousands of times — the odds literally are how often that outcome happens, minus a flat 3%. Every market returns 97%, accumulators included. No bet here is a worse deal than another.

A draw is around 1 fight in 9, stoppages a bit over a third. That's what the prices reflect.
